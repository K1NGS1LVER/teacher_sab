# Teacher-sab against current learning research

Date: 2026-11-12

## Executive judgment

Teacher-sab has a strong evidence-aligned core. Its best-supported choices are:

- retrieval before and during teaching;
- distributed review across sessions;
- worked examples followed by completion and independent problems;
- explicit corrective feedback;
- concrete examples, comparison, and application;
- self-explanation prompts;
- adaptation to prior knowledge and learner pace;
- making the structure of a lesson visible.

The main issue is calibration. The package often turns good research-backed tendencies into universal rules: probe every strand, quiz every node, use two checks for deeper nodes, require far transfer, treat foundations as unconditional truths, and use a fixed 1/3/7/14 review ladder. Research supports the direction of these rules but not their unconditionality or exact numbers.

The highest-value improvement is to make the system criterion-based and adaptive: choose the next activity from evidence of performance, delay, prior knowledge, task complexity, and the target outcome instead of from fixed ritual alone.

## Evidence scale used here

- **Strong match:** broad support across reviews, meta-analyses, or repeated classroom studies.
- **Good match with boundary conditions:** supported, but only for particular learners, materials, outcomes, or timing.
- **Plausible design choice:** sensible and compatible with learning science, but not itself established as a causal learning effect.
- **Contradiction / overclaim:** the rule is too strong, is unsupported as written, or conflicts with known boundary conditions.

## Comparison matrix

| Probe before teaching | Strong match, with conditions | Pretesting and retrieval can activate knowledge and improve later learning; probing also personalizes instruction. | Do not assume every probe improves learning. Keep it short, give feedback, and stop when the decision it informs is made. |
| Probe until the learner's edge is bracketed on every strand | Plausible, over-rigid | Prior knowledge strongly changes what guidance is useful. | There is no established need to exhaustively map every strand. Use a minimum diagnostic set and update the model from performance during teaching. |

| Dependency graph / connected knowledge | Good match with a scope limit | Organizing information and comparing examples can support schema formation and transfer. | Not all domains have a clean DAG. ## Where the package matches strongly

### Retrieval practice is the backbone

The probe, node checks, warm-up, free recall, and review queue all use retrieval rather than passive rereading. This is the strongest part of the design. A systematic review of applied classroom research found benefits across age groups, content areas, delays, and test formats, although the evidence base was still concentrated in WEIRD settings. A major review also finds retrieval benefits across materials and learner abilities.

Sources:

- Agarwal, Nunes & Blunt (2021), *Retrieval Practice Consistently Benefits Student Learning*: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9
- McDermott (2021), *Practicing Retrieval Facilitates Learning*: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010419-051019

The improvement is to distinguish three jobs that are currently sometimes collapsed into “quiz”:

1. **Diagnostic retrieval:** before teaching, to locate prior knowledge.
2. **Learning retrieval:** shortly after teaching, to strengthen the node.
3. **Assessment retrieval:** after a delay and in a changed context, to estimate durable learning.

The package has all three, but the implementation should explicitly protect the third. Passing an immediate applied check should not mark a node mastered.

### Spacing is correctly treated as a cross-session process

The review queue and next-due dates are much better than treating a lesson as complete at the end of one chat. Distributed practice has strong support, but the exact spacing schedule is not universal. The current wording that 1/3/7/14 is a starting heuristic is correct and should be made operational: choose the next interval from the learner's last delayed performance, desired retention horizon, and item difficulty.

### Worked examples and fading are well chosen

The CI example in the package is a good pattern: complete example, partial completion, then independent generation. This is especially appropriate for novices and high-element-interactivity tasks. The expertise-reversal warning is also important: the same guidance can become redundant as knowledge grows.

Sources:

- Sweller et al. (2019), *Cognitive Architecture and Instructional Design: 20 Years Later*: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5
- Renkl (2021), *The Worked Example Principle in Multimedia Learning*: https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108894333.023

The main improvement is to make “fade after understanding” observable. For example: fade when the learner can explain the purpose of each step and solve one new near-transfer case, not merely when they answer one check correctly.

### Transfer is treated as an outcome rather than assumed

The package's variable-surface cases, negative case, near/far progression, and application checks are stronger than definition-only tutoring. Research warns, however, that “far transfer” is not one outcome and should not be promised as a general consequence of learning. Transfer varies by domain, context, time delay, social setting, modality, and whether the learner must recognize or generate the principle.

Sources:

- Barnett & Ceci (2002), *When and Where Do We Apply What We Learn?*: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.128.4.612
- National Academies-style review applied to biology transfer, *Transfer: A Review for Biology and the Life Sciences*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8711802/

Use a transfer ladder and report the level reached: same example, new surface, new problem structure, delayed retrieval, or changed domain. Do not call a node “understood” merely because it passed a far-transfer item; far-transfer failure may reflect distance rather than a missing foundation.

## Main tensions and contradictions

### 1. The package sometimes confuses a good ritual with a causal mechanism

“Every node gets the full loop,” “two questions,” and “never skip the probe” make the teacher consistent, but consistency is not the same as learning benefit. Extra questions can become fatigue, and an interaction pattern can consume the time that should have gone to delayed retrieval or practice.

**Change:** retain the loop as a default, but add skip/merge conditions:

- merge tiny nodes when they share one check;
- skip a diagnostic branch when repeated performance already brackets mastery;
- replace immediate checks with a delayed cumulative check when the learner is stable;
- stop when the mastery criterion is met, not when a question quota is filled.

### 2. “Applied, never restated” can remove useful foundational retrieval

The anti-regurgitation rule correctly prevents the learner from answering by copying the paragraph above. But not every useful check must be applied. A learner may need to retrieve a term, symbol, or unconditional fact before they can apply it.

**Change:** use a staged check policy:

```text
new term/fact → direct recall
new relationship → explain or predict
new procedure → completion problem
stable concept → changed-surface application
delayed node → cumulative/interleaved transfer
```

### 3. “Unconditional truths” can create brittle knowledge

The phrase is valuable as a warning against building on an unexamined prerequisite, but real domains contain assumptions and exceptions. Teaching a simplified rule without its scope can create a misconception that later requires unlearning.

**Change:** rename the category to **working foundations** and store the scope with it:

```text
Working foundation: <claim>
Applies when: <conditions>
Breaks when: <boundary or exception>
```

For a true axiom or definitional convention, say so. For an empirical rule, include its domain of validity.

### 4. Self-explanation is treated as more settled than it is

The package is right to favor explanations that articulate causal relationships, and the 2018 meta-analysis of prompted self-explanation found a meaningful average benefit. But effects vary, and recent preregistered work found no causal advantage over rereading for fictional factual knowledge. The likely target is not “more words from the learner”; it is the generation of useful inferences.

**Change:** prompt only when there is an edge to explain:

> What changed from Node B to Node C, and why does that change make C possible?

Score the answer for required relations. Do not ask for self-explanation after every node.

Sources:

- Bisra et al. (2018), *Inducing Self-Explanation: A Meta-Analysis*: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x
- Recent preregistered factual-knowledge study, *No Causal Self-Explanation Effect for Factual Knowledge* (2026): https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.70174

### 5. The claim about cognitive-load management needs to include expertise reversal

The package mentions expertise reversal, but many of its fixed structures remain novice-oriented: complete explanations, explicit links, worked examples, and frequent checks. Those are appropriate defaults for novices but can be redundant for advanced learners.

**Change:** use learner performance to remove scaffolds. If the learner repeatedly succeeds on delayed, changed-surface tasks, shorten explanations, hide the dependency explanation unless requested, and move to problem-first practice.

### 6. Interleaving and dual coding are named but not fully implemented

The evidence base says they are available, but the current teach loop mostly uses blocked practice and uses visuals only when a graph is clearly warranted. That is not a flaw; it is more honest than forcing either technique everywhere. The skill should say this explicitly.

**Change:**

- Interleave when the learner must discriminate between similar procedures, categories, or causes.
- Keep blocked practice when the learner is still acquiring the first schema or when the procedure has high element interactivity.
- Use a visual only when it carries relational or spatial information that prose does not.

## Recommended revised teaching policy

### Before teaching

1. Read learner context and prior artifacts.
2. Ask the goal and desired retention/use case.
3. Run the smallest diagnostic set that changes the plan.
4. Identify prior knowledge, likely misconceptions, and task complexity.
5. State the plan and its mastery evidence.

### For each concept

1. Motivate the problem or decision.
2. Teach the minimum complete explanation, with scope and boundaries.
3. Give a worked example when the learner is novice or the task is complex.
4. Ask one check at the appropriate level: direct recall, explanation, completion, or application.
5. Give corrective feedback and a second attempt only when the first result indicates instability or misconception.
6. Add a short connection prompt when the relationship itself is the learning target.

### After several concepts

1. Use a cumulative, interleaved check across old and new nodes.
2. Ask for comparison when concepts could be confused.
3. Delay the next assessment enough that the learner cannot rely on the immediately preceding wording.

### At close

1. Use brief free recall or a structured brain dump.
2. Grade against a visible rubric of essential relations, not prose length.
3. Record misses, misconceptions, and confidence separately.
4. Schedule the next retrieval based on desired retention horizon and observed performance.
5. Mark a node as **introduced**, **near-transfer stable**, or **delayed-transfer stable** rather than simply learned/unlearned.

## Highest-priority improvements

### Priority 1 — Replace fixed quotas with mastery criteria

Replace “two questions for deeper nodes” with something like:

```text
Mastery for this node:
- one correct retrieval after a short delay;
- one changed-surface application;
- no unresolved misconception;
- for procedures, one successful completion or independent solution.
```

The exact criterion should vary by goal and intensity.

### Priority 2 — Add delayed cumulative retrieval inside and across courses

The current warm-up is good, but the system should deliberately mix older nodes with the current topic and record delayed performance separately from immediate checks. This is likely a larger gain than adding more explanation detail.

### Priority 3 — Make scope and exceptions explicit in foundations

Change “unconditional truth” language to “working foundation” unless the statement really is definitional or axiomatic. Add “applies when” and “breaks when” fields for empirical rules.

### Priority 4 — Calibrate scaffolding to demonstrated expertise

Use a simple state machine:

```text
novice → worked example + explicit explanation
emerging → completion problem + reduced hints
stable → independent problem + changed surface
advanced → problem first + optional explanation
```

Move backward after a miss or misconception; move forward after delayed, varied success.

### Priority 5 — Make learning claims more precise in the skill file

Replace language such as “strongest research support” or “apply every time, never skipped” with calibrated language: “default when the target and learner make it appropriate; skip or adapt when the evidence or workload calls for it.” This will make the package more scientifically defensible without weakening its teaching identity.

## Bottom line

Teacher-sab is not fundamentally at odds with learning science. Its core loop is better aligned than ordinary explanation-first tutoring. The largest gap is procedural rigidity: the package sometimes treats a good default sequence as a universal law. Make the loop adaptive, separate immediate learning checks from delayed mastery evidence, use direct retrieval before application when appropriate, and treat self-explanation, interleaving, visuals, and far transfer as targeted tools rather than mandatory ingredients.
